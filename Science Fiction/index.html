    Science Fiction¶

    This is Appendix B of the Players Handbook. The core rules — tests, combat, character creation — are the same for every setting; only the aptitudes, keywords, and equipment change.

    See Mixing Settings for running more than one at the same table.

    Long distances, thin air, and salvage worth more than the ship. Anywhere from a grimy near-future to the far end of a long expansion.

    Currency: prices use the same numbers as Section 6. A copper piece is a credit; 1 sp is ten, 1 gp is a hundred.

    What changes: the aptitude list below replaces Step 1's, the keywords replace Step 2's suggestions, and the equipment replaces Section 6's. Sections 2 through 5 are unchanged.

    B1. Aptitudes¶

    Choose 2 at character creation, as normal.

    Skill Aptitudes¶

    • Pilot: Advantage on DEX tests to fly, dock, or evade in any craft you've been checked out on. Once per long rest, attempt a manoeuvre the GM has called impossible
    • Void-Walker: Advantage on tests to work in vacuum, zero gravity, or a failing suit. You do not panic when the seal goes
    • Tech: Advantage on INT tests to repair, override, or sabotage machinery and software. Identify unfamiliar equipment's origin and condition on an INT test DC 12
    • Xenologist: Advantage on INT/WIS tests concerning non-human life, language, and artefacts. Once per long rest, declare one true thing about a creature or artefact you're examining
    • Corpsman: Stabilise a dying character with no test. Once per short rest, ten minutes of care restores 1d6 + WIS HP. Works on most non-human physiologies at disadvantage
    • Marine: No disadvantage with military weapons. Advantage on tests to hold formation, breach, or fight in low gravity
    • Salvager: Advantage to assess wreckage, find the valuable thing in a hold, or judge whether a structure is about to fail
    • Broker: Advantage on CHA to negotiate, appraise, or find a buyer. Once per adventure, name a contact at any station you've visited

    Talents: Break and Enter, Move Silently and Unseen, Silver Tongue, Athletics, and Languages work as written in Appendix A.

    Inherent Aptitudes¶

    Use Section 4's Inherent Aptitudes, read differently:

    Aptitude Reads as
    Darkvision / NightVision Ocular implant, or a species evolved somewhere dim
    Natural Armor Carapace, subdermal plating, a synthetic chassis
    Natural Weapons Claws, spurs, integrated tooling
    Tough Heavy-worlder, gene-modified, or simply built for it
    Small A low-gravity native, an engineered crew-form, a drone-body
    Wings Only meaningful in low gravity or with atmosphere — say which
    Hard to Pin Down Reflex modification

    Two Skill Aptitudes means baseline human. One or more Inherent Aptitudes means you are something else — engineered, alien, augmented, or synthetic. Your keywords say which.

    Suggested Combinations¶

    Concept Aptitudes
    Ship's pilot Pilot + Tech
    Salvage crew Salvager + Void-Walker
    Ship's doctor Corpsman + Xenologist
    Boarding marine Marine + Athletics
    Trader Broker + Silver Tongue
    Station rat Break and Enter + Move Silently
    Synthetic Natural Armor + Tough
    Contact specialist Xenologist + Languages

    B3. Gear Packs¶

    All packs include a vac suit, a comm, and 2d6+5 credits.

    Pack Contents
    Deck Suit liner (+1 AC, 2 slots), sidearm (d6, 1 slot), multitool, torch, tether
    Boarding Hardsuit (+3 AC, 5 slots), carbine (d8, 2 slots), breaching charge, tether
    Survey Suit liner (+1 AC, 2 slots), scanner, sample kit, beacon, rations (UD8)
    Salvage Suit liner (+1 AC, 2 slots), cutting torch (d6, 2 slots), prybar, patch kit, lamp

    Charge packs run on a usage die — d8 for a loaded weapon, rolled once after each fight.

    A note on energy weapons. They are not better than a rifle; they are a rifle with a different resupply problem. If your setting has both, let ballistics be cheap and repairable and let energy weapons recharge anywhere there's power. That trade is more interesting than a bigger die.

    B5. Optional Rules¶

    Vacuum. No suit means a CON test DC 12 each round. Failure costs 1d6 HP and you're unconscious on the second failure. A holed suit runs its air on a UD6 instead of a UD8, and the sound of it is the best pressure a GM has.

    Low and zero gravity. In low gravity, jumps and lifts get advantage and anything requiring traction gets disadvantage. In zero gravity, you move only by pushing off something — plan your route or drift. Void-Walker cancels the disadvantage.

    Radiation and exposure. Treat as a condition per Section 5. Severity 4-6d6 depending on source, resisted with CON, and it doesn't stop when you leave the room.

    Distance. Nothing kills a science fiction campaign faster than instant travel. Give every journey a duration in days and roll for one complication en route. A crew that can't simply leave is a crew with problems.

    B6. Tone Notes¶

    The information problem¶

    Science fiction has fantasy's problem in reverse: the characters have databases, sensors, and comms, and can in principle look up anything. Left alone, that dissolves improvised play the same way a phone does. The answers here are better than modern's, because the setting hands them to you:

    • Light-lag and distance. A query to the nearest archive takes hours or weeks. You will decide before the answer arrives.
    • The database is corporate. It contains what someone wanted in it. The redactions are the plot.
    • Nobody has been here. Survey and salvage games are built on the absence of records. The scanner tells you the mass and the temperature; it does not tell you what the thing is.
    • The sensors work fine. They report exactly what's there, and it makes no sense. This is the good one — trust the instruments and let the reading be the horror.

    Everything else¶

    Keep it grubby. These rules give a starting character 10 + CON hit points and no shields, which means science fiction Rath is Alien and Firefly, not Star Wars. The ship needs repairs, the air is a resource, and nobody is coming.

    Resist the technology arms race. The weapon list above tops out at the same d10 as a greatsword on purpose — the moment a plasma rifle does d12 because it sounds like it should, the fantasy appendix stops being playable at the same table and the whole structure of this book falls over. Make advanced technology interesting through traits and logistics, never through bigger numbers.

    The strangeness in a science fiction game should be genuinely strange. A xeno that's just an orc with more arms wastes the setting. Ask what it wants, and make the answer something a human wouldn't want.
